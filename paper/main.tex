\begin{abstract}
This study evaluates whether theoretically motivated modifications of a
stability-penalized decision tree (SPDT) improve classification
performance, robustness to label noise, or split-selection stability.
The original criterion scores a split as Gini gain minus a weighted
bootstrap variance of that gain; we test a normalized variant that
divides the penalty by $\mathrm{Gain}^2+\varepsilon$, targeting the
scale-dependence of the original penalty, and we benchmark against a
faithful re-implementation of ANLS, a published node-level bootstrap
stabilization method. All methods share identical repeated nested
cross-validation splits over eight UCI datasets (45 outer evaluations
per dataset). On clean data the normalized variant does not improve
over custom CART (3 wins, 5 losses, mean Macro-F1 difference $-0.0001$)
and is slightly below the original SPDT (2 wins, 6 losses);
the Friedman omnibus test is not significant
($\chi^2(3)=3.75$, $p=0.290$). Under training label noise of 10--20\%,
an exploratory signal favors the normalized penalty (median paired
differences up to $+0.0217$ with bootstrap confidence intervals
excluding zero), which is not confirmed by the primary omnibus
protocol. Split-selection stability does not improve (normalized
variant has the lowest root-feature agreement, $0.78$), and ANLS
achieves the highest split stability yet not better prediction
stability, empirically separating the two notions. Computational cost
of the penalized trees is modest at refit time but tuning requires
hundreds of bootstrap gain evaluations per split. We report positive,
negative, and inconclusive findings without superiority claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{ecision} trees are fast and interpretable, but their
structure is unstable: small training perturbations change the selected
split \cite{li2002instability}, motivating ensembles
\cite{breiman1996bagging,breiman2001randomforests,dietterich2000ensemble}
and stabilization methods
\cite{turney1995stability,briand2009stability,lee2024anls,mirzamomen2017stable,dannegger2000nls}.
A previous experiment of ours evaluated a candidate criterion
$\mathrm{Score}(s)=\mathrm{Gain}(s,D)-\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}_b[\mathrm{Gain}(s,D^{(b))}]$;
descriptively it ranked best among single trees yet provided no
confirmatory evidence of improvement (Friedman $p=0.1353$), did not
improve prediction stability, and cost roughly $40\times$ CART fitting
time. That formulation's penalty is scale-dependent, which hinders the
interpretation of $\lambda$.

This extension asks five questions.
\textbf{RQ1:} does a normalized bootstrap gain-variance penalty improve
Macro-F1 relative to CART and the original SPDT?
\textbf{RQ2:} do SPDT or its normalized variant resist corrupted training
labels better?
\textbf{RQ3:} does penalizing bootstrap gain variance improve
split-selection stability, even if prediction stability does not improve?
\textbf{RQ4:} are any observed benefits worth the computational cost?
\textbf{RQ5:} how does the method compare with the closest reproducible
published stabilization method (ANLS \cite{lee2024anls})?
Null hypothesis: no systematic difference across independent datasets.

\section{Related Work}
Tree stability measurement and stabilization have a long history:
quantitative stability measures \cite{turney1995stability}, tree
similarity with node-level bootstrap stabilization (NLS)
\cite{briand2009stability,dannegger2000nls}, multi-attribute split tests
\cite{mirzamomen2017stable}, and ANLS, which iterates per-node
bootstraps until a binomial $Z$-test confirms the modal predictor and
uses the median winning cutoff \cite{lee2024anls}. Modified split
criteria include Tsallis-entropy measures \cite{wang2017tsallis},
label-noise-robust criteria \cite{yang2019pairgain}, bias-corrected Gini
selection \cite{strobl2007bias}, uncertainty-gated stream splitting
\cite{domingos2000vfdt}, environment-consistent splitting
\cite{delise2026era}, and minimum-split-gain regularization
\cite{chen2016xgboost}. Coefficient-of-variation impurity measures
\cite{himabindu2015cvdt} are keyword-adjacent but use variability as the
\emph{impurity}, never as a dispersion penalty on the gain. A prior-art
search (2026-10-22) found no formula-level equivalent of
$\mathrm{Gain}-\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}_b[\mathrm{Gain}]$ or its
normalized form; a negative search is not proof of novelty, and both
formulations remain candidates.

\section{Proposed Methods}
\subsection{Original SPDT (baseline)}
For node data $D$ with Gini impurity
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Gini}(D) = 1 - \sum_{c=1}^{C} p(c\mid D)^2,
\label{eq:gini}
\end{equation}
the CART gain of a candidate split $s$ is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Gain}(s,D) = \operatorname{Gini}(D)
- \tfrac{|D_L|}{|D|}\operatorname{Gini}(D_L)
- \tfrac{|D_R|}{|D|}\operatorname{Gini}(D_R).
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
With $B$ bootstrap resamples $D^{(b)}$ of the node rows,
\begin{equation}
V(s) = \operatorname{Var}_{b}\!\left[\operatorname{Gain}(s,D^{(b)})\right],
\qquad b = 1,\dots,B,
\label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Score}(s) = \operatorname{Gain}(s,D) - \lambda V(s).
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Deterministic policies: midpoint thresholds between sorted distinct
values; validity constraints on original counts; empty resample children
contribute zero; seeded bootstrap per node. For $\lambda{=}0$ the
estimator reduces to a custom CART reference verified against an
independent implementation.
\subsection{Normalized SPDT (candidate)}
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Score}_{\mathrm{norm}}(s) =
\operatorname{Gain}(s,D)
- \lambda\,\frac{V(s)}{\operatorname{Gain}(s,D)^2 + \varepsilon},
\label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon>0$ stabilizing near-zero gains. The normalization makes
the penalty scale-free with respect to the gain magnitude, addressing
the main limitation of \eqref{eq:score}. This is an exploratory
hypothesis; it is not claimed to be superior or novel.
\subsection{ANLS Comparator}
ANLS \cite{lee2024anls} repeatedly draws bootstrap resamples at each
node, finds the plain CART-optimal split per replicate, and stops when
the binomial $Z$-statistic of the top-two predictor frequencies exceeds
$Z_\alpha$ ($\alpha{=}90\%$, minimum 10 replicates); the stabilized
split uses the modal predictor with the median of its winning cutoffs.
Our faithful re-implementation follows the paper's pseudocode with a
documented iteration cap of 400 (the paper reports median 23 and mean
364 iterations per node); all datasets are numeric, so the categorical
branch is omitted.

\section{Experimental Setup}
Datasets: the same eight UCI tabular datasets as the baseline study
(Table~\ref{tab:inventory}); sources and SHA256 hashes are unchanged.
Design: repeated stratified nested CV, outer 5 folds $\times$ 3 repeats
on seeds $[42,123,2026]$ (45 evaluations per dataset--method, identical
splits for all methods), inner stratified 3-fold tuning; median
imputation fitted on training folds only.

\emph{Tuning budget (staged, documented).} Stage S1 tunes the tree
structure on plain custom CART ($4{\times}3{\times}3$ configurations);
S2 and S3 tune $\lambda\in\{0.1,1,10\}$ for the original and normalized
criteria at the frozen structure with $B{=}10$ and $\varepsilon{=}10^{-4}$.
After a pilot on three datasets, the predefined budget was reduced and
frozen: random forest context benchmark uses 100 trees; two noise
realizations per level; eight stability subsamples; the ANLS iteration
cap above. All decisions used training folds only.

\emph{Label noise protocol.} Outer training labels are corrupted at
levels $\{0,5,10,20,30\}\%$ (each corrupted label moves to a different
class; deterministic seeds; identical corruption masks across methods;
two realizations per level). Hyperparameters are frozen from
clean-label inner CV; outer test folds remain clean.
\emph{Stability protocol.} Per outer fold, eight models are trained on
90\% subsamples with frozen parameters; we measure pairwise root-feature
agreement, root-threshold variation within the modal feature only, and
prediction disagreement on the fixed outer test fold. The unit of
inference is the dataset.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Baseline Recap (Separate Experiment)}
The original study reports SPDT versus custom CART: 6 wins, 0 ties,
2 losses, mean Macro-F1 difference $+0.0058$; Friedman
$\chi^2(2)=4.0000$, $p=0.1353$, Kendall's $W=0.2500$ (not significant);
SPDT prediction disagreement $0.1014$ versus $0.0928$ (custom CART);
mean fitting time $21.43$ s versus $0.53$ s per outer fold. Those
artifacts are preserved unchanged.
\subsection{Clean-Data Comparison (Extension)}
Table~\ref{tab:extperf} reports per-dataset Macro-F1 for six methods.
The normalized variant does not improve over custom CART (3 wins, 5
losses, mean difference $-0.0001$) and is slightly below the original
SPDT (2 wins, 6 losses, $-0.0029$). ANLS is descriptively the best
single tree (5 wins, 3 losses, $+0.0066$), comparable in magnitude to
the original SPDT's baseline gain. The Friedman omnibus over the four
primary methods is not significant ($\chi^2(3)=3.75$, $p=0.290$), so
confirmatory post-hoc tests were not run; paired analyses are
exploratory with bootstrap confidence intervals.

\section{Robustness Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:noise} and Fig.~\ref{fig:noise} show Macro-F1 against
training label noise. All methods degrade; the random forest remains
the most robust reference. The normalized penalty degrades the least
among single trees: median paired differences against custom CART are
$+0.0063$ (5\%), $+0.0136$ (10\%, CI $[0.0015,0.0186]$), $+0.0217$
(20\%, CI $[0.0132,0.0333]$), and $+0.0121$ (30\%, CI $[-0.0051,0.0280]$).
Per-level Friedman tests are significant at 10\% ($p=0.024$) and 20\%
($p=0.003$). These noise analyses are exploratory: they were not the
primary confirmatory path, multiple levels were examined, and the
high-noise interval includes zero. We therefore report a promising but
unconfirmed robustness signal, not a robustness claim.

\section{Stability Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:stability} and Figs.~\ref{fig:rootagr}--\ref{fig:predis}
summarize stability. Penalizing bootstrap gain variance does
\emph{not} improve split selection: the normalized variant shows the
lowest root-feature agreement ($0.78$ versus $0.83$ for CART variants)
and its prediction disagreement ($0.105$) exceeds both CART ($0.093$)
and the original SPDT ($0.096$). ANLS attains the highest split
agreement ($0.85$, consistent with its design) yet its prediction
disagreement is the highest among single trees ($0.109$): stable splits
do not guarantee stable predictions, and the two notions must be
reported separately. Root-threshold variation is small for all methods
and is only computed within the modal feature.

\section{Ablation Study}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} (identical splits, frozen structure) shows
that all penalty configurations cluster within $\pm0.013$ Macro-F1;
the normalized variant with small $\lambda{=}0.1$ ranks highest
($0.8617$) while large $\lambda$ or small $\varepsilon$ hurts slightly;
depth restrictions (depth 3, $0.835$--$0.836$) matter far more than any
penalty setting. Ordinary tree-complexity control, not the variance
penalty, dominates the outcome variance.

\section{Computational Cost}
Table~\ref{tab:runtime} reports refit times of tuned configurations and
counters from the estimators. A penalized refit evaluates about $18$k
candidate splits and, when $\lambda>0$, roughly $178$k bootstrap gain
evaluations per outer fold; refit times remain under $0.1$ s, ANLS
averages $2.2$ s, and a single-process memory probe measured $2.3$ MB
peak for a penalized fit. These figures exclude the staged tuning
overhead (S1--S3, tens of inner-CV fits per outer fold), which is the
dominant cost of the penalized methods and must be budgeted in
practice. Gains of the size observed do not justify this overhead for
clean-data deployment; the judgment is closer under label noise, where
the exploratory signal appeared.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{RQ1:} the normalized penalty does not improve clean-data
Macro-F1; it performs at or slightly below CART and the original SPDT,
and the omnibus test finds no differences ($p=0.290$).
\textbf{RQ2:} under 10--20\% training label noise the normalized
variant degrades least among single trees, with paired confidence
intervals excluding zero; this exploratory signal requires independent
confirmation before any robustness claim.
\textbf{RQ3:} neither the original nor the normalized penalty improves
split-selection stability; the normalized variant is the least stable
at the root split, and prediction stability mirrors the baseline
finding (slightly worse than CART). ANLS confirms that split stability
and prediction stability are distinct quantities.
\textbf{RQ4:} refit-time cost is modest, but tuning overhead is
substantial; the observed clean-data effects do not justify the cost,
while the noise-robustness signal is the only result that begins to do
so.
\textbf{RQ5:} ANLS, faithfully re-implemented, is descriptively the
strongest single-tree comparator and a more promising stabilization
avenue than gain-variance penalties on these datasets.

\section{Limitations}
Eight small datasets limit statistical power; Gini impurity only;
numeric features only; a single disagreement-based stability proxy;
noise injected independently of features (real label noise is often
not uniform); frozen clean-label tuning under noise is one of several
defensible protocols; ANLS uses a capped iteration budget; the closest
alternative criteria (minimax split, conditional inference trees) were
analyzed but not benchmarked; and the novelty of both penalty
formulations remains unverified at the formula level.

\section{Conclusion}
Across eight UCI datasets with leakage-free repeated nested CV, a
normalized bootstrap gain-variance penalty did not improve clean-data
classification over CART or the original SPDT, did not improve split or
prediction stability, and adds tuning overhead. Its one promising
signal---slower degradation under 10--20\% training label noise---is
exploratory and awaits confirmatory replication on larger benchmarks.
A faithful ANLS implementation was descriptively the best single-tree
stabilized method, and empirically separated split stability from
prediction stability. We conclude that, under the tested conditions,
simple CART remains preferable for clean data, and that future
stabilization research should prioritize voting-based per-node
stabilization over gain-variance penalties.
